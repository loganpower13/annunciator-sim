Engine Ice Protection System Components
Inertial Vane System

Prevents engine compressor inlet screen from accumulating ice. When ice vanes are lowered, they deflect the airstream slightly downward, creating a venturi effect. At the same time, an inertial vane bypass door under the cowling is also opened, allowing an exit for ice particles.

Ice particles, due to greater mass and momentum, accelerate past the screen area and vent overboard through the bypass door.

Engine Air Inlet Lips

Heated by engine exhaust gases to prevent ice formation. On airplanes BB-1266, BL-129 and subsequent, hot engine exhaust flows from the left stack, through the lip, and exits out the right stack.

NOTE: The engine air inlet lip heating system is automatic and does not require pilot action.
Control Systems

Prior to BB-1444, except 1439: ICE VANE toggle switches with mechanical backup T-handles

BB-1439, 1444 and subsequent: ENG ANTI-ICE toggle switches with dual actuators and standby system

Circuit breakers located on copilot's right side panel in engine group.

Annunciator Lights

Green: L/R ENG ANTI-ICE or L/R ICE VANE EXT - indicates vane and bypass door in extended position

Amber/Yellow: L/R ENG ANTI-ICE or L/R ICE VANE - indicates system did not operate to desired position within 15 seconds

System Limitations
Operating Temperatures

Extension: When operating in visible moisture at +5°C or colder

Retraction: At +15°C and above to ensure adequate engine oil cooling

CAUTION: If in doubt, extend the vanes. Engine icing can occur even though no surface icing is present. If freedom from visible moisture cannot be assured, engine ice protection should be activated.
Performance Impact
IMPORTANT: Lowering the ice vanes will result in a slight ITT rise and a significant loss of torque at normal cruise power settings. Normal torque may be regained with the power levers, observing the ITT limits.
Manual System Operation (Prior to BB-1444, except 1439)
Mechanical Backup

Actuated by pulling T-handles just below the pilot's subpanel. Airspeed should be decreased to 160 knots or less to reduce forces opposing manual operation.

CRITICAL WARNING: Once the manual override system has been engaged (i.e., any time the manual ice vane T-handle has been pulled out), do not attempt to retract or extend the ice vanes electrically, even if the T-handle has been pushed back in, until the override linkage in the engine compartment has been properly reset on the ground.

However, the pilot can raise or lower the vanes repeatedly, any time, with the manual system engaged.

Electrical System Coordination

During manual system use, the electric switch position should match the manual handle position for correct annunciator indication, provided the appropriate circuit breaker has been pulled via the checklist.

When the vane is successfully positioned with the manual system, the amber annunciator light(s) will extinguish.

Standby System Operation (BB-1439, 1444 and subsequent)
Dual Actuator System

Backup system consists of dual actuators and controls. Illumination of the L and R ENG ANTI-ICE (amber) annunciators indicates that the system did not operate to the desired position.

Immediate illumination
Indicates loss of electrical power
Delayed illumination
Indicates inoperative actuator
In either event
STANDBY actuator should be selected
Operational Considerations
Icing Conditions Protocol

During flight in icing conditions, the following systems should be activated:

  • Engine ice vanes extended
  • Fuel vent heat ON
  • Pitot heat ON
  • Prop deice ON
  • Windshield heat ON
  • Stall warning heat ON
NOTE: Operation of strobe lights will sometimes show ice crystals not normally visible.
Vane Position Verification

The vanes must be either retracted or extended. There are no intermediate positions.

A reliable backup check on the position is to closely monitor engine torque. Normal torque may be regained with the power levers, observing the ITT limits.
