Electrical System Components
DC Generators

Two 30-volt, regulated to 28.25 ± .25 volts, 250-ampere starter-generators connected in parallel provide normal DC power. Either generator can supply the entire electrical load.

Optional 300-ampere starter-generators are available and installed on some airplanes.

Generator Control Units (GCU)

Maintain constant voltage during variations in engine speed and electrical load requirements. The voltage regulating circuit will automatically connect or disconnect a generator's output to the bus.

NOTE: Generators will drop off the line if underexcitation, overexcitation, overvoltage, or undervoltage conditions exist.
Volt-Loadmeters

Located on the overhead panel, these indicate generator amperage in percent of 250 amps per generator. The associated meter button must be pressed to indicate bus voltage.

Generator Switches

Located on the pilot's left subpanel under the MASTER SWITCH gang bar with the BAT switch. To turn the generator on, the control switch must be held upward in the GEN RESET position for a minimum of one second, then released to the ON position.

System Limitations
Generator Load Limits

Maximum sustained generator load is limited as follows:

  • Sea Level to 31,000 feet altitude: 100% (250 amps)
  • Above 31,000 feet altitude: 88% (220 amps)
  • Ground Operation: 85% (212.5 amps)
Single Generator Operation
IMPORTANT: When operating with only one generator, monitor the loadmeter of the operating generator and do not exceed 100% load (88% above 31,000 feet). Reduce electrical load if necessary by turning off non-essential equipment.
Electrical Distribution System
Bus Architecture

The electrical system uses a dual bus architecture with four sub-buses fed by both the left and right generator buses:

  • No. 1 and No. 2 Dual Fed Bus - Copilot's side panel
  • No. 3 and No. 4 Dual Fed Bus - Fuel panel circuit breaker bus

Each subbus is fed from either side through a 60-ampere current limiter, a 70-ampere reverse current diode, and a 50-ampere circuit breaker.

Isolation Bus

When the battery, generators, or GPU are providing power, the isolation bus, L generator bus, and R generator bus function as one unit, as long as both current limiters are not open.
